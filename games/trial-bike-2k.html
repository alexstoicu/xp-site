trial bike 2k

» play trial bike 2k (free, no ads, version 1.0)

why

Long story short: Trial Bike 2K is my version of a blast from the past, Trial Bike, the Java applet game from the early 2000s. Same idea, new engine, more chickens.

It's a game, but it's also one of my first experiments in vibecoding, so it might not suit everyone's taste. Rough edges come included, at no extra charge. If it's not for you, no hard feelings: Dummy Dan has taken worse bonks.

what it is

A little dirt-bike game you play in your web browser. Ride over hills, hay bales and machines without falling off. If your head bumps the ground, that's a "bonk", and you start again from the last flag.

what's in it

  • Driving School: Dummy Dan, a crash-test dummy, teaches you to go, stop, lean, wheelie and hop. Pass his test and you get your Rider's Permit.
  • Level 1, Farm Delivery: hay bales, a wobbly see-saw, a hay wagon and the roof of a barn. Bring a rubber chicken to the farmer.
  • Level 2, Quarry Whistles: down into a big quarry, up a digger's arm, and hop from post to post over the water.
  • Level 3, Robot's Socks: a giant magnet grabs your bike and sticks it to the ceiling, so you ride upside down over a factory full of robots and hot melted metal.

Three stars per level: finish, no bonks, and beat Dummy Dan's time. There are badges to collect, and a 2-player mode: pass the phone and beat each other's time.

how it was made

Made with Claude, an AI helper. I said what I wanted, played each new version, and said what to fix ("the bike looks jumpy", "the chimney is floating", "add more hay"). Claude wrote the code, drew all the pictures with code, made the sounds and music, and fixed things.

tricks behind the scenes

  • Real physics: the bike has springs and wheels that really bounce, like a toy car.
  • Robot testers: before each new version, little robot players rode every level to check it could be beaten. Some careful, some sloppy, some stopping all the time, like real kids.
  • The upside-down trick: the computer secretly runs the ceiling as a normal floor, then shows it to you upside down. That's why riding on the ceiling still feels right.
  • Smooth riding: the game draws the bike in between its physics steps, so it glides instead of jumping.
  • One file: the whole game, with pictures, sounds and music, fits in one web page.
